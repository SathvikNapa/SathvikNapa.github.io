Research interests

Four areas organize my research. Each starts from a question I want to answer, and each connects to the work listed on this page.

AI alignment and safety

How do models behave under pressure, and can we measure it?

A model that answers correctly in a neutral chat can give the answer up when a user pushes back. A single push shows that sycophancy exists, but not how it scales, so I study how behavior changes as the pressure grows. A related question is whether a model can tell when its own reasoning has been edited, since chains of thought are routinely edited for safety and evaluation.

I also look at model uncertainty, using hidden-state classifiers together with external consistency checks to catch hallucinations.

Related: SycophancyDosageResponse, SycophancyEval, chain-of-thought tampering paper

Language and multimodal modeling

What do speech and other modalities carry that text drops?

People in a good conversation drift toward each other's tone and emotion, and speech carries timing, overlap, dialect, and stance that a transcript loses. I work on models that read this context: who is talking, to whom, and what came before.

The same question runs the other way for machines. Current speech-to-speech models score well on audio quality and speech recognition, and still differ from people in timing, overlap, dialect, emotional adaptation, and stance.

Related: TRACE, TRACE dataset, SPEARBench

Multi-agent systems

What happens when agents act and coordinate on their own?

I build multi-agent systems, such as an extraction pipeline on LangGraph and AWS Bedrock with schema enforcement and data-quality checks. I want to understand where systems of agents fail, drift, or disagree, how much oversight they need, and how to evaluate them before they are deployed.

Recursive self-improvement

What happens when a system changes itself?

Recursive self-improvement is, in practice, a reinforcement learning question. A system that writes its own tasks, scores its own outputs, or trains on its own data is optimizing against a signal it helped define. I want to understand what that signal ends up rewarding, what drifts when it is optimized hard, and whether anyone watching can still tell.

The chain-of-thought study is the closest work so far: reasoning traces are one of the few things we can read while a model is being optimized, so it matters whether they can be altered unnoticed.

Related: chain-of-thought tampering paper

I apply this work where mistakes are costly: healthcare, social systems, and education. See Applied ML.

Papers

Can Reasoning Models Detect Changes to Their Chains of Thought?

EMNLP 2026arXivCode

5 modelsown vs other models' chainsedits after and during reasoning
  • Question. Editing a model's chain of thought is a routine intervention, for example to prefill reasoning from a stronger model or to remove steps that lead to unsafe output. Can the model tell that its chain was changed?
  • Approach. Chains of thought were modified and shown back to the model, both once reasoning was complete and partway through it. Models were tested on their own chains and on chains written by other models.
  • Result. Detection accuracy is modest. Models also struggle to say how a chain was changed, and they notice changes to their own chains about as well as changes to other models' chains.

TRACE: Temporal Relationship-Aware Conversational Entrainment Detection in Dyadic Speech

IEEE SLT 2026arXivDatasetCodeWebsite

97.01% accuracy300 h of speech
  • Question. As speech agents spread, can a system tell whether two speakers are emotionally coordinating, given who they are to each other and what was said before?
  • Approach. TRACE reads a conversation as an ordered sequence of acoustic windows, using embeddings from an emotion fine-tuned Whisper model, instead of pooling each utterance. It also uses conversational context and the relationship between the speakers. The DyadEE dataset pairs real conversations with synthetic ones where entrainment is broken by swapping partners or resynthesizing emotion.
  • Result. Context and relationship information both improve detection, and TRACE reaches 97.01% accuracy on DyadEE.

SPEARBench: A Benchmark for Naturalness Evaluation in Streaming Speech-to-Speech Language Models

IEEE SLT 2026arXivWebsite

open benchmarkweb platform
  • Question. Standard speech and text benchmarks miss whether a speech-to-speech model behaves naturally in conversation: timing, turn-taking, prosody, stance, and dialect all matter.
  • Approach. Controlled two-turn question-answer prompts from the Seamless Interaction corpus are run through several models. Answers are scored on latency, interruptions, speech quality, ASR robustness, dialect consistency, emotional naturalness, and interpersonal stance, with human answers as the reference.
  • Result. Current models reach high signal quality and low ASR error, yet still differ from people in latency, overlap, dialect preservation, emotional adaptation, and stance.
  • Limits. Evaluation is automatic, English-only, and limited to two speakers. A public site lets others compare systems and submit models.
